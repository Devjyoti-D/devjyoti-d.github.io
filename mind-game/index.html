Mirror

You are not playing against the AI. You are playing against what the AI believes you will do.

A colony of 100 learning agents shares four regions. You never control any of them: you shape their world with one intervention per season. While you try to read them, each agent is quietly building its own model of you, and acting on it.

      How it works

      1. A social dilemma

        In each region, competitors grab twice a cooperator's share, but competition wastes the land. Grabbing pays for one agent and hurts everyone together, as in many real commons.

      2. They model you

        Each agent learns, only from your past moves, what you tend to do next (a Markov model with forgetting) and which rule you use to pick a region: the crowd, the competitors, the richest, the poorest, the same as last time...

      3. They act on it

        Before your move lands, every agent picks a region and a stance to maximise its expected payoff under its forecast of you. If they expect an audit in Riverside, its competitors quietly move elsewhere.

      4. They hide

        Agents caught out while doing the obvious thing, or who watch a big catch, make their own choices more random. Exploit their predictability and they learn to become unpredictable.

      The idea behind it

      • This is a toy version of theory of mind and opponent modelling: two learners modelling each other. Being predictable makes your moves cheap to counter, which is why randomising (a mixed strategy, in game-theory terms) is often optimal.
      • Each agent is different: how fast it forgets, how much it trusts its model of you, and how strongly it believes cooperation pays. So the colony's forecast is the average of 100 slightly different minds.
      • Every game uses a new random seed: different land, different agents, and a random event part-way through.

      Everything runs in your browser in plain JavaScript; no data is sent anywhere.
